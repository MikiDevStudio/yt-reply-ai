02the panel

Everything is a control, not a setting you go and find.

The panel opens over the comment you are answering and every decision is made in it — 460 pixels wide, no page to navigate to, and nothing that moves under the cursor while a reply arrives.

Five tones, one of them automatic

Auto matches the comment you are answering. Friendly, humorous, engaging and brief override it. Changing a chip regenerates on the spot.

autofriendlyhumorousengagingbrief

Creativity, as five dots

Plain, grounded, natural, inventive, bold — each carries its own instruction and its own temperature, because telling a model to be bolder without raising the temperature changes nothing.

creativity

Channel or viewer

The same voice, two standings. As the channel you answer for the channel; as a viewer you speak only for yourself, and the model is told the difference.

channelviewer

Any language, detected or pinned

The reply follows the comment’s own language by default. Type a language into the header and it is used until you clear it — useful when your audience writes in three.

English

A note, per reply

What you know that the page does not: a fact to include, a link to mention, or a rough draft to be fixed up rather than replaced.

mention the follow-up is filmed already

Retries you can compare

Every attempt is kept and paged through with ‹ 2/3 ›. Each retry is given a different angle and a step more room than the last, so the second answer is a different answer — not the same one reworded.

03soul profile

The difference between a reply and your reply.

built from questions

Answer a handful of them and the profile writes itself: what the channel is, how you sound, how long a reply should run, whether emoji are welcome, and the words that sound like you rather than like a brand.

a questionAnswer it, or say honestly that the answer is a whole video.
criticismTake the point. Argue only where there is something to argue.
an argumentOne calm line, or nothing at all — your rule, set once.
or write it yourself

Already have a voice document, a style guide, or a prompt you use elsewhere? Paste it. The extension can tighten it or reshape one written for another tool, and you can read exactly what gets sent before anything is.

it stays here

The profile lives in this browser’s extension storage. It is not synced to a Google account, not backed up by us, and not readable by any page — including YouTube.

what actually gets sent

A screen in settings shows the exact prompt for a real comment: profile, tone, context, note. No hidden system message, no telemetry rider.

04context & cost

You decide how much it knows, and pay for exactly that.

Context is tiered, because context is what a reply costs. Most comments need none of it — and the tier that does costs a fraction of a cent, once per video rather than once per comment.

caching

The video’s context is read once and reused for every comment under it, so the shared part of the prompt is billed at a fraction on repeat calls.

retries think harder

The first attempt is held to minimal reasoning; pressing retry says the obvious answer missed, so the next one is allowed to look for a different one — and the token count is shown, never hidden.

no youtube api

Titles and descriptions are read from the page you already have open. No API key of YouTube’s, no quota to run out of, nothing to authorise.

06privacy

No server is ever in the path of a reply.

no account

Nothing to sign up for, nothing to log into, no identifier of any kind. The extension has no way to know that you exist.

no telemetry

No usage pings, no error reports, no install beacon. Two hosts are reachable at all and the manifest is scoped so no third can be: openrouter.ai, where the replies are written, and one of ours — touched at most twice in the life of an install, and only when you press something.

your key, your terms

The key is stored on your device, spends only what you cap it at, and can be revoked at OpenRouter in one click.

Ours is contacted twice at most: once for the free trial key, once to turn a supporter code into a licence. Neither carries a word of anybody's comment, neither happens unless you press something, and after them the extension never speaks to us again — a licence is checked against a key that ships inside the extension, so it keeps working whatever happens to us.

The full policy — every stored item, every field of every request — is on the privacy page, and the code that implements it is public.

08questions

The awkward ones, answered first.

Is there a limit?+

No. There was one — 50 replies a day — and it was removed: the only thing it measured was how many people it stopped in the middle of a comment section. What is left is a card at the foot of the reply popover every fiftieth reply, under the answer that just arrived — a sentence and a coffee button. It stands in nothing’s way, it closes with its own X, it reports nothing, and a coffee switches it off for good. That is the whole of what the free version costs.

What does it cost to run?+

Whatever the model costs, paid to OpenRouter at cost with no markup from us. Measured on the default model: about $0.002 for a reply, and a little more for a retry, which thinks harder on purpose. There are free models in the picker too — they cost nothing and are capped by OpenRouter at 20 requests a minute.

Do you see my comments?+

No, and there is no place for them to arrive: nothing of ours sits in that path. The comment goes from your browser to OpenRouter on your own key, and to whichever model provider you picked. We do have one service — it hands a new install its trial key and turns a supporter code into a licence — and it never sees a comment, a reply or a request to generate one.

Will it post replies automatically?+

Never. The generated text is put in YouTube’s reply box and stops there. Pressing Reply is yours — which is both the honest design and the right side of the Web Store’s rules on automated engagement.

Does it work in YouTube Studio?+

Yes. The same button appears in Studio’s comment inbox, which is where most channel owners actually answer from.

What if the reply is wrong?+

Retry with a different tone, raise the creativity, or write a note telling it what to say. Every attempt is kept, so you can compare them and pick one — or throw all three away and type your own. It is a draft, not a decision.
